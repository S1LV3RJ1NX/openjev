\paragraph{Loading a model.} The adapter is merged into the base weights
on load, so no extra inference cost is paid by accident.

\begin{verbatim}
from openjev import DecisionModel, Choice, Noul
model = DecisionModel.from_pretrained("s1lv3rj1nx/openjev-router-lora")
out = model.answer(state, {"intent": Choice(...), "clinical": Noul(...)})
out["intent"].label, out["intent"].probabilities
\end{verbatim}

\section{Reproducing the main results}
\label{app:repro}

\begin{center}
\small
\begin{tabular}{ll}
\toprule
result & command \\
\midrule
Held-out transfer & \texttt{scripts/eval\_heldout.py --ckpt <ckpt>} \\
Router against reference & \texttt{scripts/eval\_router.py --ckpt <ckpt> --dump o.json} \\
Paired McNemar & \texttt{scripts/compare\_to\_jev.py --ours o.json} \\
 & \quad \texttt{--jev baselines/jev\_healthcare\_router.json} \\
Latency & \texttt{scripts/bench\_latency.py --ckpt <ckpt>} \\
Data audit & \texttt{scripts/audit\_data.py <task dir> --deep} \\
Packing check & \texttt{scripts/check\_packing.py <mixture>} \\
Binary diagnosis & \texttt{scripts/diagnose\_binary.py --ckpt <ckpt>} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The reference system's per-item predictions on all 450 router test items
ship in the repository as
\texttt{baselines/jev\_healthcare\_router.json}. A McNemar test needs both
systems' answers on the same items, so publishing only aggregate
accuracies would let a reader check our deltas but not our significance
claims. That file is one pass against a non-deterministic and evolving
service, so it is the baseline we measured rather than a fixed property
of the product.
